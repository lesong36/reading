# RE Foundations / 7A / Understanding Dreams

Our dreams come from a part of the brain that contains our thoughts and memories. A person can have up to six dreams a night. Each one usually lasts from 10 to 40 minutes. Everyone dreams, but not everyone remembers their dreams. Most people dream in color, usually with sound. And we usually dream about ourselves and the people we know. Why Do We Dream?

Alan Siegel is a scientist who studies dreams. “[Dreams] can tell us a lot about ourselves,” he says, “and can help us figure out problems.” Another scientist, Robert Stickgold, thinks dreams come from our memories. Stickgold says that dreaming about past events is useful because it helps us learn from them. Here are a few types of dreams and what people think they mean.
